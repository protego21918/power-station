Downed line and generator safety.

Treat every downed line as energized

Stay at least 35 feet back, keep others away, and call it in. Do not touch a line or anything it's resting on, a fence, a car, standing water, even if it looks dead.

  • Digging

    Call 811 before you dig

    The state one-call center marks buried lines within your property free of charge, usually within a few business days.

  • Generators

    Never connect a generator without a transfer switch

    Plugging a generator directly into your home's wiring can back-feed power onto the grid and injure a line crew working on what they believe is a dead line.

  • Wildlife

    Report animal contact with equipment

    Squirrels and birds on transformers are a leading cause of small, localized outages. Report sightings near substations to the safety line.

How storm restoration is prioritized.

Crews work in this order, whether it's ten outages or ten thousand.

  1. First

    Critical infrastructure

    Hospitals, water treatment, and 911 dispatch are restored first, regardless of customer count.

  2. Second

    Substations and trunk lines

    Repairing the lines that feed the most customers restores power to entire neighborhoods at once.

  3. Third

    Neighborhood branch lines

    Smaller lines serving blocks and streets are repaired next, in order of how many customers each one affects.

  4. Fourth

    Individual service lines

    Repairs to a single home or business, often a damaged weatherhead or service drop, are handled last.
